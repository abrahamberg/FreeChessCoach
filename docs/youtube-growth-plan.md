# FreeChess Coach on YouTube: why the channel is not growing, and the plan

Written 2026-10-24, eleven days after the first upload; revised the same
day after the owner's correction: **the app is free and will stay free,
nobody pays for a chess app in a crowded market, and the bet is to earn
money on YouTube itself** to support the app and the people behind it.
This is a marketing diagnosis, not a code plan: the *why* and the *what*
first, the *how* last. Numbers come from the owner's YouTube Studio
summary (2026-10-24), the channel's public feed, the live courses at
freechesscoach.org, and the content studio (`chess-content-studio`, pushed
2026-10-15).

## 1. The short version

The channel has a reach problem that is not a reach problem. Four thousand
Shorts views in eleven days is a normal start. What is broken is
everything that turns a view into a subscriber and a subscriber into
watch time, and watch time is the only thing YouTube pays for:

1. **No one knows who the channel is for or who is talking.** Three
   audiences (people who have never played, 1000-rated trap hunters,
   people who want an AI coach) and nine different hosts in sixteen
   uploads. A subscription is a bet on the next video; with no consistent
   promise the bet is never placed. 1,500 unique viewers, 3 subscribers.
2. **The long video is the course, filmed.** Eight minutes of speech, up
   to ten chapter cards, every defence in lecture order. Average view
   duration of 1:25 on 6–8 minute videos is about 20% retention; YouTube
   stops recommending at that level, and 1:25 of a video earns almost
   nothing even once ads are on.
3. **The packaging is a rendered slide, not a thumbnail.** Nine long
   videos use one identical layout: a full 32-piece board, the site name,
   three lines of mid-sized text, a small portrait. At phone size nothing
   reads. 10,494 impressions, 1.18% click-through: YouTube showed the
   videos to ten thousand people and 124 clicked.
4. **Publishing has no quality gate.** A long video titled "#shorts". A
   Short whose title is its file name ("punish the halloween gambit
   reel"). A long video whose description is two alternative title drafts
   in quotes. Short descriptions with an empty "Full lesson:" placeholder.
   Three videos uploaded within thirty minutes, twice. One upload at
   02:00. Chapters promised in every description, added to none.
5. **The goal and the calls to action point off YouTube.** "1,000,000
   views" is a vanity number (93% Shorts views are worth almost nothing),
   and every video ends by sending the viewer to the website. If the money
   is on YouTube, the viewer has to stay on YouTube: subscribe, watch the
   next video, come back Tuesday.

The plan: one viewer, one host, long-form as the product, Shorts as its
trailer, a thumbnail system, a publishing checklist, formats chosen for
watch time per minute of production, and a revenue roadmap with honest
numbers. Section 6 has the fixes to make this week, including rewrites of
all fifteen live titles.

## 2. What YouTube actually pays for (the economics behind every decision)

Ballpark figures for an English-language chess education channel; treat
as ranges, not promises.

| Source | Gate | What it pays |
|---|---|---|
| Ads on long videos | Partner Program: 1,000 subscribers **and** 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days) | roughly $2–6 per 1,000 long-form views; videos of **8 minutes or more** can carry mid-roll ads and earn about twice as much per view |
| Ads on Shorts | same | about $0.03–0.10 per 1,000 views. 10M Shorts views ≈ a few hundred dollars. Shorts are not a money format; they are the trailer. |
| Memberships, Super Thanks | the lower tier (500 subscribers, 3,000 watch hours or 3M Shorts views, where available) | tens to a few hundred dollars a month under 10k subscribers |
| Sponsorships | in practice 10,000+ subscribers and a reliable 10,000+ views per long video | the real money in mid-size chess channels: about $20–40 per 1,000 views for an integrated read, so $400–1,000 per video at 20–30k views |
| Affiliates, donations | none | small from day one: Chessable and board/book affiliates in the description; "support the free coach" (Ko-fi, GitHub Sponsors). The free-app story is a good donation story. |

What this means:

- **Long-form watch time is the product.** Everything else is a dial.
  4,000 watch hours is 240,000 minutes: at an average view of 3 minutes
  that is 80,000 long-form views in a year, about 220 a day. A channel
  with 4% click-through and 40% retention gets there in 5–7 months; the
  channel as it is today never does.
- **Length follows retention, not the other way round.** A 12-minute
  video nobody finishes earns less than a 5-minute one people finish. Fix
  retention at 5–7 minutes first (Section 4.4), then grow to 8–12 minutes
  with structures that hold attention (Section 4.6) and switch on
  mid-rolls.
- **Realistic size.** Good execution for 12 months in this niche is a
  channel of 10–30k subscribers and 100–300k long-form views a month:
  about $300–1,000 a month in ads plus the first sponsorships. Twenty-four
  months with a few hits: 300–500k long views a month, $1–2.5k in ads and
  one or two integrations a month, $3–6k a month in total. That is the
  upside case that "supports the app and ourselves". The median faceless
  education channel at 24 months is well below it. The difference is
  packaging, retention and consistency, which is what this plan is about.

### 2.1 The risk that can end the bet: the Partner Program's "inauthentic content" rule

Since July 2025 YouTube refuses or removes monetization for channels whose
content it judges **mass-produced or repetitive**: templated videos,
synthetic narration over near-identical visuals, little original value.
This channel is AI-written, AI-voiced, and today renders every video from
one visual template. Educational content with real substance has been
fine; the pattern "same layout, same voice, same structure, daily" is
exactly what a reviewer flags. This must be designed against from now,
not fixed after a rejection:

- **Original substance in every video:** engine-verified lines, numbers
  ("one in five players around 1000 play this"), the human defences, a
  real viewer's game (4.6). The studio's chess checking is an asset here;
  say it on screen.
- **One character, openly an AI coach,** with a name, a personality and a
  consistent voice, presented as what it is ("The Huntress, the AI coach
  from freechesscoach.org"). Honest, differentiating, and not a fake
  person. Tick YouTube's "altered or synthetic content" disclosure on
  every upload; it costs nothing.
- **Visual variety across videos:** thumbnails that differ (4.5), board
  crops and camera framing that change, on-board labels instead of the
  same chapter card, different cold opens.
- **The owner's voice on the hook and the outro** (thirty seconds per
  video, AI for the body) is the single strongest mitigation and also the
  single strongest subscriber-conversion lever: people subscribe to
  people. It is the owner's call; if the answer is no, the one-character
  route above is the minimum.

## 4. The plan: what the channel becomes

### 4.1 Positioning

> **FreeChess Coach:** where players under 1500 learn to spring the traps
> and punish the gambits they keep losing to. Every line engine-checked.

One viewer: the 700–1400 online player. One tone: sharp, confident, a
little predatory, never a lecture. The free app is the channel's unfair
advantage, not its product: it makes a verified video for almost nothing
(volume and consistency are the strongest predictors of channel growth),
and it can review any real game on request (4.6), which no competitor can
automate.

### 4.2 Decisions

1. **Long-form is the product. Shorts are its trailer. The course is the
   production engine and a reason to come back.** The call-to-action
   order in every video: subscribe (at the first payoff) → the next video
   (end screen) → the course (description and pinned comment). The spoken
   outro no longer sends people away; it says what is on Tuesday.
2. **One host on YouTube: The Huntress.** She already fronts three of the
   five trap courses and the best-performing Short; "hunt / prey / stalk"
   gives the series its language. Other coaches stay in the app. Revisit
   after 20 videos, not before.
3. **Chess From Zero stays on the site, off the YouTube calendar.** Keep
   the three uploaded lessons public in a playlist, post no more of them.
4. **Formats, in order of watch time per hour of production** (4.6): the
   single trap (now), the trap compilation (build it next), the viewer's
   game reviewed (the flagship once the audience exists), the 10-minute
   opening. One slot in five stays an experiment.
5. **The subscribe ask moves to the first payoff** (about a third in,
   right after the mate or the won queen), is one sentence, and is a
   promise with a schedule: *"Every Tuesday and Friday: one trap players
   under 1500 fall for, engine-checked. Subscribe so you are the one
   springing it."*
6. **North star: long-form watch hours and subscribers** until the Partner
   Program; then long-form views × RPM; then sponsorship readiness (10k
   subscribers, 10k views per video median). Views in total are not a
   goal.

### 4.3 The Short, rebuilt (its job: subscribers and the bridge to the long video)

- **Frame one:** the bait position and the question on the top band,
  before any move: "Free queen. Take it?" / "Would you take this?"
- **Spoken first sentence** names the opening and the stake in under
  eight words: "Stafford Gambit. White just took my queen."
- **Build-up ≤ 8 seconds.** The reel timeline today plays build-up from
  second 3 to second 20 at 650 ms a move; most of the swipe-away happens
  there. Moves before the bait at 350–400 ms, the bait held, the
  punishment slowed.
- **Climax by second 12–15. Total 25–40 seconds.** Short enough to loop.
- **No spoilers in the title or the top band.** "The queen mate on f7"
  told the viewer the ending before they pressed play.
- **The call to action is a question, not a follow:** "Would you have
  taken it? Yes or no, below." Comments and rewatches are what Shorts
  rank on.
- **Related video → the long video, every time** (the field in YouTube
  Studio); the description's first line: "Full video (8 min): …".
- **Title:** a question or a stake, the opening in brackets, no hashtags:
  *"Would you take this free queen? (Stafford Gambit)"*.

### 4.4 The long video, rebuilt (its job: watch time)

- **Cold open ≤ 8 seconds, ≤ 20 words,** first word on frame one over the
  climax board. Title text may appear *after* the sentence lands; nothing
  slides in during it.
- **Payoff every 60–90 seconds.** The trap straight through (90 s) → why
  the bait looks right (60 s) → the subscribe promise → each human defence
  as a mini-story with its own cliffhanger ("most players run here; it
  loses faster") → the next trap on the end screen. The course's extra
  lines are not cut; they are *re-ordered* into payoffs, with the rare
  ones left to the course in one spoken line.
- **Phase 1 (now): 5–7 minutes,** until the average percentage viewed
  passes 40%. **Phase 2: 8–12 minutes with mid-rolls,** using the
  structures in 4.6. Never length for its own sake.
- **Chapter cards become one-second labels** on the board (no whoosh, no
  read-aloud). Chapters live in the description, where they also earn key
  moments in Google search.
- **Title:** the search phrase first, the twist second, ≤ 55 characters,
  no hashtags, never a file name. *"Legal's Mate: Hang Your Queen, Mate in
  Two"*.
- **Description:** line one repeats the search phrase and the stake; then
  chapters; then the next video; then the course link; affiliate and
  support links; at most three hashtags at the end.

### 4.5 The thumbnail system

Today's thumbnail is the video's title slide: correct for a slide, wrong
for a 168-pixel rectangle competing with faces and red arrows. Rules:

1. **One face, big, with an emotion.** The coach portrait fills at least
   40% of the height on one side. Each coach needs three or four
   expression variants (shocked, smug, pointing, "oh no"); one neutral
   portrait cannot carry a channel.
2. **A cropped board, not the whole board.** A 4×4 or 5×5 window around
   the critical square, at most six pieces, one red or yellow arrow or X,
   the key piece outlined. Thirty-two pieces at phone size is texture.
3. **At most three words,** cap height ≥ 160 px at 1280 wide, yellow or
   white on dark, with a stroke. *TAKE MY QUEEN?* works; *Punish the
   Englund Gambit: hunt the greedy queen* does not.
4. **No URL, no logo, no site name.** The channel name sits under every
   title already; those pixels belong to the face and the arrow.
5. **Vary the composition within one look.** A series needs a recognizable
   family, not nine identical rectangles: alternate face left / face
   right, board tilt, background colour per series. (This is also the
   repetitive-content defence.)
6. **Two variants per upload** through YouTube's own *Test & compare*.
   Target ≥ 4% click-through in browse within 30 days; a video under 2%
   after 1,000 impressions gets a new thumbnail and title within 48 hours.

### 4.6 Formats ranked by watch time per hour of production

| Format | Why it earns | Length | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Single trap** ("Punish the Englund Gambit") | Search-driven, evergreen, the app makes it today | 5–7 min now, 8–10 later | The restructure in 4.4 |
| **Trap compilation** ("5 queen traps every 1200 falls for") | The highest-retention long format in chess (5.7M and 2.7M in the studio's own research): each segment is short, each has a payoff, so 10–15 minutes hold. **Zero new research**: five existing courses stitched. Mid-rolls. | 10–15 min | A compilation template: shared cold open, one segment per course from its reel span plus the main line, one-second labels between |
| **A viewer's game, reviewed** ("Guess the rating: 'I fell for my own trap'") | The most proven chess format there is ("Guess the Elo", tens of millions). Real stakes, real person, comments guess the rating, the reveal at the end holds people to the last minute. **It is the app's core feature on camera**, and every video is unique, which is the strongest answer to the mass-produced rule. | 8–12 min | A submission route (pinned comment + form: Lichess/Chess.com link, permission to show the username or anonymise); the `master_game` storytelling template on the submitted PGN with Game Review's own verdicts; rating revealed at the end |
| **10-minute opening** ("Learn the Vienna in 10 minutes") | 5–7M-view format; the `opening` kind exists | 8–12 min | Pacing from 4.4; the plan, the traps inside as payoffs |
| **Free queen? Shorts** | Subscribers and the bridge, not money | 25–40 s | 4.3 |

Build order for the owner: the compilation template first (weeks, not
months; it doubles revenue per view and reuses everything), the viewer
game second (when there are a few hundred subscribers to submit games),
the thumbnail tool alongside both.

### 4.7 Publishing hygiene (the gate)

Nothing is uploaded until this list is ticked, by a second pass that did
not write the text:

- Title ≤ 55 characters, search phrase first, no hashtags, not a file name,
  not a draft note; Shorts titles are a question or a stake.
- Description: first line keyword + stake; long videos have chapters (0:00
  first) and the next video's link; Shorts link the long video and have
  Related video set; the course link, the support links; ≤ 3 hashtags at
  the end.
- Custom thumbnail (not the auto frame), two variants in test.
- End screen: next video first, the playlist second. Pinned comment: the
  question for the comments.
- "Altered or synthetic content" ticked. Not made for kids.
- One upload per slot. Shorts daily at the fixed hour; long videos Tuesday
  and Friday at the fixed hour (17:00 CET reaches the US morning and the
  European evening). Never three uploads in one half hour: they split the
  impressions test and look like a dump.
- The coach on screen is the channel's host.

### 4.8 Measurement

- Per video, weekly, in the studio's state: impressions, click-through,
  average percentage viewed, **watch hours**, Shorts "viewed vs swiped
  away", subscribers gained, end-screen clicks. A running total of watch
  hours and subscribers against the Partner Program gates.
- Links out still carry `?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=short|video&utm_campaign=<slug>`,
  so the site knows what the channel sends it (today no link carries a
  tag and the public pages carry no analytics I could find).
- Decision rules, on medians over at least five videos per format:
  click-through < 2% after 1,000 impressions → repackage within 48 hours;
  average percentage viewed < 30% → restructure the next video of that
  format; a Short over 5× the median → a long video from that course next
  and a second Short from another moment of it; a long video over 5× the
  median → a compilation that includes it, and a sequel.

Two honest notes:

- **This is a volume-and-consistency business.** The channel's edge is
  that a verified video costs it almost nothing to make. Two long videos

- **The gates are serial.** Nothing earns before 1,000 subscribers and
  4,000 hours, so the first 90 days (Section 6) are a subscriber and
  watch-hour sprint, and every decision is scored on those two numbers.

## 7. What the app and the studio need to change

For the owner (app), in priority order for revenue:

1. **A compilation template**: N courses → one 10–15 minute video with a
   shared cold open, a segment per course (its reel span plus the main
   line), one-second labels between, one end card. Doubles revenue per
   view through mid-rolls, reuses every course made.
2. **A thumbnail tool, separate from the title slide**: climax board
   cropped to the critical window, ≤ 6 pieces, one arrow, ≤ 3 words,
   portrait variant, two outputs per course.
3. **The viewer-game video**: the `master_game` template on a submitted
   PGN with Game Review's verdicts and the rating revealed at the end; a
   submission form; username shown only with permission.
4. **Reel timeline**: build-up ≤ 8 s, question on the top band from frame
   one, climax by 12–15 s, 25–40 s total; the CTA field becomes a comment
   prompt.
5. **Video timeline**: hook text after the spoken hook, no slide-in during
   it; silent one-second chapter labels; the subscribe line as its own
   segment after the first payoff; the end card leads with the next video.
6. **Portrait variants** per coach (three or four expressions), and visual
   variety between videos (background, crop, framing).
7. **Source tags** on generated links, and `clipLinks` filled.

## The Short

## 8. What this plan does not do

- It does not promise numbers. The 30/60/90 targets are what a channel
  that fixes packaging and retention typically reaches; the only
  guarantee is that the current packaging will not.
- It does not pretend Shorts make money. They make subscribers and send
  viewers to the videos that do.
- It does not change the free app. Links to it stay for trust and return
  visits; nothing in the plan asks a viewer to pay for it.
- It does not touch TikTok or Instagram beyond "same Short, same formula,
  link in bio". YouTube is where the money and the subscribers are.
