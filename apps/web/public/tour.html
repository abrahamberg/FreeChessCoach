Free, works straight away

Four things that work the moment you sign in

No key, no card, nothing to set up. These run on the engine and on your own games.

1. Bring your games in

Pull your recent games from Lichess or Chess.com by username, paste a PGN, or open a PGN file. The import page also warns you before the library limit would ever delete an old game.

  • Up to 30 imports a day and 150 a week, 10 analysing at a time.
  • Your library holds 1,000 games. Older games can be deleted without losing your stats, because they are kept in a weekly archive.
  • After a batch import, the app recommends the most tactical game to coach.

Try the import page in the live demo →

2. Stats that build up over time

Every analysed game feeds a set of statistics that grow with you: an estimated rating for each game, how you do in each opening, which tactics you find and which you stop, how you handle pawn structure, space and defence, and how you convert endgames.

  • An estimated-rating trend placed on the day each game was played.
  • Performance by opening, with win rate and accuracy.
  • Tactics you found, and tactics you prevented.
  • Strategy scores and endgame results by material type.

Sample data: 3,420 games over a year.

Explore these stats in the live demo →

3. Game review, step by step

Go through any game one move at a time. Each move is labelled, an evaluation graph shows where the game turned, and tactics are explained in plain language: what you missed, what you allowed, and what the better move was.

  • Available for every game you import, with no daily cap on reviews.
  • Try alternative moves on the board and see how the position changes.
  • One click from a game to a coaching session, if you have connected an AI key.

Open a game review in the live demo →

4. Bots that lose the way people lose

Thirty bots across five levels, from a newcomer rated around 300 to an expert around 2300. Instead of a strong engine with its power turned down, each bot plays good moves most of the time and then slips the way a person of that level does, with its own blind spots and personality.

  • Practise against realistic mistakes: hung pieces, missed forks, forgotten threats.
  • Choose a time control, take moves back, and review the game afterwards like any other.
  • No AI key needed.

Free coach, you plug in the AI

What the AI coach adds

The coach is free from us. The AI it thinks with is a separate thing you connect yourself, like your own internet subscription: you pick the provider, and you decide what to spend, which can be nothing on a free plan. We add nothing on top. Read how keys are handled or follow the setup steps.

1. A coaching session on your own game

Pick a game you imported and the coach walks through it with you. It asks before it tells, so you learn to see the position yourself, and every claim it makes about a position is grounded in the engine's analysis instead of guessed.

  • Moves on the board follow the conversation.
  • A choice of coach personalities, from patient to demanding, with the same method and honesty.
  • Optional spoken delivery.

Try a coaching session in the live demo (the reply is pre-written) →

2. A diagnosis, not a slogan

The coach looks for why you keep making the same mistake, and turns it into a focus area you can act on. Up to three are active at a time, so you are never handed a list of twenty things to fix.

  • Measured diagnoses, worked out from your analysed games, show how often each pattern fails and how sure the app is. The coach explains them and works on them with you.
  • Focus areas move from needs attention to improving to resolved as you change.
  • Each note ends in one thing to do, not a lecture.

See a diagnosis in the live demo →

Is "Fails to run CCT" a good diagnosis?

No. It is a terrible diagnosis. "Failing to run CCT" is not a diagnosis; it is a symptom. It is the coaching equivalent of a doctor saying, "My diagnosis is that you failed to stay healthy."

Telling a student "You need to check for Checks, Captures, and Threats" is useless because every 800-rated player already knows they should do it. They aren't failing because they lack the knowledge of CCT; they are failing because a psychological or mechanical barrier prevents them from executing it.

3. Trends across your games

One game is an anecdote. Across many, the coach can see what keeps coming back and whether it is fading. The Progress page shows your mistake trends over your last five and twenty games, and a history of recent lessons with the homework the coach set.

  • See which mistake types are rising or falling.
  • Each lesson keeps its date, the game it covered and a one-line summary.
  • Practice puzzle sets are picked to match your strongest diagnoses.

Sample data: a player six weeks in, rated in the 800s.

See the trends in the live demo →

4. Play with the coach

This is not a game for fun. It is a lesson you play: you move, and the coach talks about most of them, asks what you saw, and helps you understand each position as it happens. Three to seven of these a week is the pace the app is built for.

  • The coach plays the other side and explains as you go.
  • Take a move back when you see a mistake.
  • Finished games join your history like any other.
