User guide

Your first week with FreeChessCoach.

Everything here is free from us. Up to step 6 you need no AI at all; steps 7 to 9 add the coach, which runs on an AI service you connect yourself. Screenshots use sample data from a made-up player.

1 Sign in · 2 Link accounts · 3 Import · 4 Review · 5 Stats · 6 Bots · 7 AI key · 8 Coaching · 9 Progress · The weekly routine

  1. Sign in with Google

    Choose Sign in with Google. There is no separate account form and no credit card. From Google we receive your email address and the name on your profile, and never your password. You need to be at least 16 to use the service (see the Terms).

    You land on Games, which starts empty. The top bar has four places: Games, Play, Progress and Stats. Once you have games, this page shows the ones you imported, your practice sets and any coaching in progress.

    See the Games page in the live demo →

  2. Link your Lichess or Chess.com account

    Open Settings and find Linked accounts. Enter your Lichess and/or Chess.com username. This lets the import page list your recent games without you pasting anything. You only ever enter a public username, never a password for those sites.

  3. Import your games

    Open Import a game. Choose a source: From Lichess, From Chess.com, paste a PGN, or upload a file.

    For a single game you pick one of two buttons:

    • Analyze is the free move-by-move review. No AI needed.
    • Get coaching session starts a session with the AI coach. This one needs an AI key (step 7).

    To bring in many games at once, select several from your Lichess or Chess.com list. You can import 30 a day and 150 a week, with up to 10 analysing at a time, and the app shows what is left. When a batch finishes, it points out the game with the most tactics as the best one to coach.

    Try the import page in the live demo →

  4. Review a game, move by move

    When analysis finishes, open the game from Games and choose Review. Step through the moves on the board. Each move is labelled, the graph shows where the evaluation swung, and the notes explain missed and allowed tactics in plain words. Try a different move on the board to see what would have happened.

    Review is free for every game you import.

    Open a game review in the live demo →

  5. Watch your stats build up

    Every analysed game feeds Stats: estimated rating over time, openings, tactics found and prevented, strategy and endgames. Use the range buttons (last 7 days, 30 days, year, all time) and switch between Rapid and All formats. The numbers get more meaningful after a few dozen games.

    Your library holds up to 1,000 games. If you delete old ones, or the app makes room, your stats stay, because deleted games are folded into a weekly archive first.

    Explore the stats in the live demo →

  6. Play a bot that makes human mistakes

    Open Play, then Play a bot. Filter by skill tier, pick a bot, choose a time control and start. Bots play like people of their level, so you practise against realistic mistakes. Finished games are reviewed like any imported game. No AI key needed.

    See the bot picker in the live demo →

  7. Optional: connect your AI key

    The coach runs on a key you bring. Before you do, read how the key is handled. Then follow the OpenAI walkthrough: create an account, set a small spending limit, make a key just for this app, and paste it into Settings → AI setup.

    Use a key with a small monthly limit, about $5 to begin with, and adjust it to what you actually use.

    See the AI setup section in the live demo →

  8. Have your first coaching session

    Pick a game you care about, ideally a serious one with tactics in it, and choose Get coaching session (or Coach on a game you already imported). Enter your unlock phrase when asked.

    The coach asks before it tells. Answer in your own words, and use the board to show what you saw. You can end the session whenever you like, and pick it up later.

    To play a learning game instead, open Play and choose Play the coach, then Play as White or Play as Black. The coach follows the game live.

    Try a coaching session in the live demo (the reply is pre-written) →

  9. Follow your focus areas on Progress

    Progress shows this week's focus, your active focus areas with the coach's notes, measured diagnoses, mistake trends, and a list of recent lessons with their homework. Aim for one thing at a time: fix the primary focus area and the rest follows.

    See the Progress page in the live demo →

The routine

What a good week looks like

This is the pattern FreeChessCoach is designed around. You don't have to follow it exactly, but it is what we would do.

~10

games a day

Ten-minute rapid on Lichess or Chess.com, against real people.

1

coached game

Import the day's games, then coach the serious one with the most tactical moments.

20

minutes of puzzles

On Lichess, using the theme your coach recommends, or a mix if it has none yet.

3–7

games with the coach

Learning games where most moves are discussed, spread across the week.

Ten-minute rapid gives you time to think, which is what makes a game worth reviewing. Blitz produces many games but few worth coaching.
