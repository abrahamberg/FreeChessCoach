User guide

Your first week with FreeChessCoach.

Everything here is free from us. Up to step 6 you need no AI at all; steps 7 to 9 add the coach, which runs on an AI service you connect yourself. Step 10 is an optional voice for the coach, with three ways to set it up. Screenshots use sample data from a made-up player.

  1. Sign in with Google

    Choose Sign in with Google. There is no separate account form and no credit card. From Google we receive your email address and the name on your profile, and never your password. You need to be at least 16 to use the service (see the Terms).

    You land on Games, which starts empty. The top bar has four places: Games, Play, Progress and Stats. Once you have games, this page shows the ones you imported, your practice sets and any coaching in progress.

    See the Games page in the live demo →

  2. Link your Lichess or Chess.com account

    Open Settings and find Linked accounts. Enter your Lichess and/or Chess.com username. On Lichess it is the name at the top right when you are logged in, and on Chess.com it is the end of your profile address (chess.com/member/username). This lets the import page list your recent games without you pasting anything. You only ever enter a public username, never a password for those sites.

  3. Import your games

    Open Import a game. Choose a source: From Lichess, From Chess.com, paste a PGN, or open a PGN file.

    For a single game you pick one of two buttons:

    • Analyze is the free move-by-move review. No AI needed.
    • Get coaching session starts a session with the AI coach. This one needs an AI key (step 7).

    To bring in many games at once, select several from your Lichess or Chess.com list. You can import 30 a day and 150 a week, with up to 10 analysing at a time, and the app shows what is left. When a batch finishes, it points out the game with the most tactics as the best one to coach.

    Try the import page in the live demo →

  4. Review a game, move by move

    When analysis finishes, open the game from Games and choose Review. Step through the moves on the board. Each move is labelled, the graph shows where the evaluation swung, and the notes explain missed and allowed tactics in plain words. Try a different move on the board to see what would have happened.

    Review is free for every game you import.

    Open a game review in the live demo →

  5. Watch your stats build up

    Every analysed game feeds Stats: estimated rating over time, openings, tactics found and prevented, strategy and endgames. Use the range buttons (last 7 days, 30 days, year, all time) and switch between Rapid and All formats. The numbers get more meaningful after a few dozen games.

    Your library holds up to 1,000 games. If you delete old ones, or the app makes room, your stats stay, because deleted games are folded into a weekly archive first.

    Explore the stats in the live demo →

  6. Play a bot that makes human mistakes

    Open Play, then Play a bot. Filter by skill tier, pick a bot, choose a time control and start. Bots play like people of their level, so you practise against realistic mistakes. Finished games are reviewed like any imported game. No AI key needed.

    See the bot picker in the live demo →

  7. Optional: connect your AI key

    The coach runs on an AI you bring: either a cloud key, or a model running on your own computer (LM Studio or Ollama). For a cloud key, before you do, read how the key is handled. Then follow the OpenAI walkthrough: create an account, make a project just for this app, turn on data sharing for it (which gives free daily tokens, at a privacy cost the walkthrough explains), set a monthly limit with Enforce hard limit, make a key, and paste it into Settings → AI setup, then pick an unlock phrase the form accepts as strong. Already have an Anthropic or OpenRouter key? Choose it under Provider there instead; the app fills in the rest. The welcome guide shown after your first sign-in walks through the same steps. To use a local model instead, choose On my computer (LM Studio / Ollama) there and follow the local walkthrough.

    Use a key with a small monthly limit, about $5 to begin with, and adjust it to what you actually use.

    See the AI setup section in the live demo →

  8. Have your first coaching session

    Pick a game you care about, ideally a serious one with tactics in it, and choose Get coaching session (or Coach on a game you already imported). Enter your unlock phrase when asked.

    The coach asks before it tells. Answer in your own words, and use the board to show what you saw. You can end the session whenever you like, and pick it up later.

    To play a learning game instead, open Play and choose Play the coach, then Play as White or Play as Black. The coach follows the game live.

    Try a coaching session in the live demo (the reply is pre-written) →

  9. Follow your focus areas on Progress

    Progress shows this week's focus, your active focus areas with the coach's notes, measured diagnoses, mistake trends, and a list of recent lessons with their homework. Aim for one thing at a time: fix the primary focus area and the rest follows.

    See the Progress page in the live demo →

  10. Optional: give the coach a voice

    The coach can read its replies aloud. There are three ways to do it. Each one is folded away below; open only the one you want. The voice is off until you switch it on in Settings → Coach voice. Each option there is described in plain words, and if you have no OpenAI voice model set up, the free Device voice is ticked for you, and each coach has its own voice on every option.

    Option 1: OpenAI voice (easiest, uses your own OpenAI key)

    If your AI setup uses OpenAI (or a compatible API under Other) and you added a voice model there, choose OpenAI voice in Settings → Coach voice. It is fast and needs nothing installed. It is billed to your own OpenAI account, the same way the coach is. If you did not set up a voice model in AI setup, this option is greyed out.

    Option 2: Browser voice (free, nothing to install, but slow)

    Choose Browser voice in Settings → Coach voice. The voice runs entirely inside your browser, so nothing leaves your computer and it costs nothing. The first use downloads a voice model. It is slow: how slow depends on your computer, and it is generally much slower than the other options. Keep the tab open while it speaks. It is still in beta.

    Option 3: Local voice server (free and fast, advanced one-time install)

    This is the most technical option, and you can skip it. It is a small voice program called Kokoro that you run on your own computer, next to LM Studio. It sounds natural, costs nothing, and nothing is sent to us or to any cloud. It needs about 10 minutes of setup once; after that it is a single switch in Settings. If commands and terminals are unfamiliar, use Option 1 or 2 instead.

    Pick one of the two ways below. Docker is easier; the terminal way avoids a large download.

    Way 1: Docker (Windows and Mac, easiest)

    1. Install Docker Desktop for Windows or Mac and open it. Wait until it says it is running.
    2. Open a terminal: PowerShell on Windows, Terminal on Mac. Paste this and press Enter:
    docker run -d --name kokoro --restart unless-stopped -p 8880:8880 ghcr.io/remsky/kokoro-fastapi-cpu:latest

    The first run downloads about 5 GB, then needs a minute to start. That is the one-time part. From then on it starts by itself whenever Docker Desktop is running. To stop or start it by hand: docker stop kokoro and docker start kokoro.

    Way 2: The terminal, no Docker

    Windows (PowerShell):

    1. Install Git, then eSpeak NG (download the Windows .msi installer and keep the default install location).
    2. Install uv by running winget install --id=astral-sh.uv -e, then close and reopen PowerShell.
    3. Download and start the voice server:
    git clone https://github.com/remsky/Kokoro-FastAPI.git
cd Kokoro-FastAPI
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\start-cpu.ps1

    Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer):

    1. Install Homebrew if you don't have it, then run brew install git uv espeak-ng.
    2. Download and start the voice server:
    git clone https://github.com/remsky/Kokoro-FastAPI.git
cd Kokoro-FastAPI
./start-gpu_mac.sh

    On an Intel Mac, use Docker (Way 1) instead.

    The first start downloads the packages and the voice model, which takes a few minutes. Leave the terminal window open while you use the coach, the same way you leave LM Studio running. Press Ctrl+C in the window to stop it, and run the last command again to start it another day.

    Turn it on

    1. Open Settings → Coach voice and switch on Enable coach voice.
    2. Choose Local voice server and confirm.
    3. Press Test voice. You should hear the coach say the server is connected. If you do, you are done.
    Advanced: use a different address or port

    The voice server normally listens on port 8880, which is what the app expects. If something else on your computer already uses that port, start the server on another one, then tell the app.

    Docker: change the first number after -p. For port 9000, remove the old container with docker rm -f kokoro and run:

    docker run -d --name kokoro --restart unless-stopped -p 9000:8880 ghcr.io/remsky/kokoro-fastapi-cpu:latest

    Mac terminal: put the port in front of the command: PORT=9000 ./start-gpu_mac.sh

    Windows terminal: the start script always uses 8880. To use another port, use the Docker way.

    Then open Settings → Coach voice, expand Advanced: use a different address or port, type the new port (for port 9000, just 9000) and press Test voice. The app shows the full address it will use. It is remembered on this device only.

    Another computer or a phone: the address is used by the browser you are in, so on a phone localhost means the phone itself. Run the voice server on a computer and type that computer's address instead, for example 192.168.1.20:8880. Leave out the port and the app uses port 80 for any address other than localhost, which uses 8880. If the app is opened over https, the voice server address must be https too, or the browser will block it. Clear the field to go back to http://localhost:8880.

    Test voice says it can't reach the server

    Check that the server is running: for Docker, docker ps should list kokoro (open Docker Desktop if it doesn't); for the terminal way, the window must still be open. Give it a minute after starting, because it loads the voice model first. Make sure the address in Settings matches where you started it. If it still fails, some browsers (Safari in particular) may stop a website from talking to your own computer, so try Chrome, Edge or Firefox.

    Kokoro is separate open-source software from its own project, not something we run or support. On an ordinary computer without a graphics card it speaks at about the pace of natural speech, so a long reply may pause between sentences. The project also publishes faster versions for NVIDIA and AMD graphics cards; those share the card with LM Studio.

The routine

What a good week looks like

This is the pattern FreeChessCoach is designed around. You don't have to follow it exactly, but it is what we would do.

~10

games a day

Ten-minute rapid on Lichess or Chess.com, against real people.

1

coached game

Import the day's games, then coach the serious one with the most tactical moments.

20

minutes of puzzles

On Lichess, using the theme your coach recommends, or a mix if it has none yet.

3–7

games with the coach

Learning games where most moves are discussed, spread across the week.

Ten-minute rapid gives you time to think, which is what makes a game worth reviewing. Blitz produces many games but few worth coaching.
